# BH Swing — Weekend-Flatten Test on Historical Equities (v1)

**Status:** Design (not yet executed). Compute and analysis pending droplet provisioning.

**Date locked:** 2026-05-21

**Methodological prior:** [`project_weekend_flatten_test.md`](../../memory/project_weekend_flatten_test.md) — forex H4 v2 weekend-flatten test, REJECTED 2026-05-07. That test explicitly contrasted forex (symmetric noise) with equities (asymmetric gap risk), but never measured the equity case. This is that measurement.

---

## The question, precisely

> Across a statistically significant sample of equity swing trades generated by the production `BaselineStrategy` and `MeanReversionStrategy` signals over 2015-01 through 2026-05, does forcing a Friday-close exit and a Monday-open re-entry produce a net positive change in cumulative time-adjusted return after accounting for: (a) opportunity cost of capital between Friday and Monday, (b) realized Sunday-gap slippage avoided, and (c) re-entry slippage incurred?

The answer can land three ways:

- **Positive:** weekend-gap tail risk is real and the rule pays for itself. We ship Friday-flatten as part of `bh_swing` before any Phase 3 live capital.
- **Negative:** opportunity cost of skipping Mon-Fri compounding outweighs avoided gap losses (forex outcome). We document the no-flatten policy as evidence-backed for equities too.
- **Regime-dependent:** rule pays during high-vol/news-heavy regimes (2018 vol spike, 2020 COVID, 2022 bear) and costs during trend regimes (2017, 2023-26). We ship a regime-gated flatten policy or surface this as an operator toggle.

## Methodology

Mirrors the forex memo's structure: simulate the flatten rule *on top of* an already-generated baseline trade ledger, rather than re-running the strategy with the rule embedded. This is computationally cheaper, methodologically cleaner (one shared trade population, three variant outcomes), and the forex precedent is already validated.

### Phase 1 — Baseline trade ledger generation

Run the existing `Backtester` (`src/bluehorseshoe/analysis/backtest.py`) across the locked universe × horizon × both strategies. Persist every trade with full leg-level detail:

```

    "entry_date":       <date>,
    "entry_price":      <float>,
    "t1_target":        <float>,
    "t2_target":        <float>,
    "stop_price":       <float>,
    "qty_t1":           <int>,
    "qty_t2":           <int>,
    "t1_exit_date":     <date | null>,
    "t1_exit_price":    <float | null>,
    "t1_exit_reason":   "target" | "stop" | "timeout" | null,
    "t2_exit_date":     <date | null>,
    "t2_exit_price":    <float | null>,
    "t2_exit_reason":   "target" | "stop" | "timeout" | null,

    "realized_r":       <float>,

```

Output: `research/weekend_flatten_equities_v1/baseline_ledger.csv`.

### Phase 2 — Flatten rule simulators

#### Flatten rule A: Uniform Friday-close exit

For every trade in the baseline ledger that spans at least one weekend:

1. At each Friday close inside the trade's lifetime, force-exit the position at the day's *closing* price (from DuckDB OHLCV). Realize that segment's P&L.
2. The following Monday: re-evaluate the original entry signal on that day's bar.
   - If signal still valid → re-enter at Monday's open price.
   - If signal no longer valid → close the trade entirely; no further segments.
3. Re-applied each subsequent Friday until either an original-bracket exit (T1/T2/stop hit during a Mon-Fri segment) or the trade times out.

Output: alternate ledger with split segments + re-entry decisions. Aggregate back to per-original-trade outcomes for comparison.

#### Flatten rule B: Asymmetric "flatten winners only"

Identical to rule A *except* at each Friday close:

- If unrealized P&L on the still-held shares is **positive** → flatten (banking the gain, avoiding weekend gap-down risk).
- If unrealized P&L is **zero or negative** → let ride with the original stop in place.

This is the rule Brand actually applies manually in equities: take profit before weekend, never re-enter a losing position after a Friday flatten (because you didn't flatten it).

Both rule files: `research/weekend_flatten_equities_v1/simulate_uniform.py` and `simulate_asymmetric.py`.

## Computational plan

Rough estimate of the baseline ledger generation:

- Universe size (post-filter): ~3,000 symbols
- Horizon: 11.3 years × ~252 trading days/year ≈ 2,850 daily bars per symbol
- Indicator stack cost: dominated by TA-Lib calls, ~5ms per symbol per bar
- Strategies: 2 (run serially against the same indicator computations)

Back-of-envelope: 3,000 × 2,850 × 5ms × 2 = ~85,500 seconds = **~24 wall-hours single-threaded**. With 8-core parallelism by symbol (the natural axis), **~3 wall-hours**.

The flatten simulators (Phase 2) operate on the ledger; cost is O(N_trades × N_weekends_spanned), trivially seconds.

Phase 3 analysis is pandas groupby; seconds.

## Success criteria for the study itself

This study is "successful" (i.e., we trust the result enough to act on it) if all of the following hold:

1. Baseline ledger contains **≥ 10,000 trades** across both strategies. Below that and statistical power for tail-risk estimation is shaky.
2. **≥ 50%** of those trades span at least one weekend (otherwise the rule barely applies and the test is moot).
3. Each major regime (2018-vol, 2020-COVID, 2022-bear, 2023-26-trend) has **≥ 1,000 trades** — enough to stratify without small-sample artifacts.
4. The result is **stable under bootstrap** (1000-resample): the sign of the cum-R delta between baseline and each flatten rule should not flip in > 5% of resamples.

If any of those fails, document the failure mode and either expand the universe / horizon, or report the result with explicit caveats.

## Decision rule for shipping a flatten policy

A flatten rule ships into `bh_swing` (before Phase 3 live capital) **iff**:

- It improves the time-adjusted return (mean R / day held) **OR** reduces max drawdown by ≥ 25% with cum-R cost ≤ 15%, **AND**
- The improvement is stable under bootstrap (per criterion 4 above), **AND**
- The improvement is not confined to a single regime (or, if it is, the regime is identifiable in real-time so a conditional rule is implementable).

## Risks and caveats

1. **Intraday data unavailable.** The Backtester operates on daily bars. Friday-flatten uses Friday close; Monday re-entry uses Monday open. Both are available in DuckDB. We do *not* simulate intraday Friday afternoon (e.g., "flatten 30 min before close" vs "at close"). This is conservative — real Friday-afternoon flattens would likely capture marginally better exits.
2. **Slippage and commissions modeled as zero by default.** The forex test was explicit about this. Equity slippage on liquid names (our filter cuts micro-caps) is on the order of 1-3 basis points; commissions at IBKR are similar. We can add a 5bp round-trip cost as a robustness check in Phase 3.
3. **Survivorship bias in the universe.** DuckDB's symbol coverage reflects today's NASDAQ; symbols delisted before 2026 may be under-represented. The Backtester's per-symbol logic handles missing-data gracefully but the universe itself is biased toward survivors. Documented limitation; not fixable in this study.
4. **Regime dependence is the most likely confounding finding.** The rising_3bar memo flagged the same: 2023-26 mean R differs strongly from 2016-23 mean R for that signal. If this study finds a similar split, the policy recommendation becomes "use a regime detector, not an always-on flatten rule" — which is a bigger architectural ask. Phase 4 work, not Phase 3.
5. **The "still valid signal on Monday" check for re-entry policy** depends on the same code path that generated the original signal. This is correct by construction but means if the original signal has a look-ahead bug, both arms inherit it. The forex test ran into this and worked around it by frozen-data evaluation; same pattern here.

## Why this matters now

Phase 3 (`bh_swing` live with $500 capital cap, per `synthetic-cooking-meerkat.md`) is several weeks out but on the locked roadmap. Going live without a measured weekend policy means either:

- Holding through weekends by default (current behavior) — exposing real capital to a tail risk that has *not* been quantified for our specific signal/universe combination.
- Adding a Friday-flatten by default "to be safe" — costing potentially measurable cum R for a benefit that may or may not exist on this signal/universe.

Either default is a guess. This study replaces the guess with evidence before any capital is at risk. The forex precedent gave us the methodology; we just need to re-run it for our actual deployment universe.
